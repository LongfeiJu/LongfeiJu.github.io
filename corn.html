Sheet 01 / 08Origin

The field

Most corn traded between countries comes from four places: the US Corn Belt, Brazil, Argentina and Ukraine. The two hemispheres harvest at different times, so new supply reaches the market twice a year.

Sheet 02 / 08Harvest

The ear

Each plant usually carries one main ear. It is harvested once the kernels dry down in the field, then dried to about 15% moisture so it keeps in storage.

Sheet 03 / 08First mile

Farm to elevator

A combine strips and shells the ears in one pass. Trucks haul the grain to a local elevator, which pays a cash price quoted as a spread to futures. That spread is the basis.

Cash price
futures + basis
Benchmark
CBOT corn, ¢ / bu
One truck
≈ 25 t ≈ 1,000 bu
Sheet 04 / 08Elevator

Storage

A bucket elevator lifts the grain and drops it into bins. Storage lets the owner pick when to sell. If later futures pay more than storage costs (a carry), the grain waits. If nearby prices are higher (an inverse), it moves now.

Key ratio
stocks-to-use
Balance sheet
USDA WASDE, monthly
Sheet 05 / 08Export terminal

To port

Grain reaches export terminals by barge, rail or truck. Most US Gulf exports come down the Mississippi on barges. At the terminal, conveyor galleries feed a shiploader that fills each hold in turn, and the ship settles deeper as it loads.

FOB
price loaded on board
CIF / CFR
FOB + freight (+ insurance)
Sheet 06 / 08Dry bulk

Ship sizes

Corn moves in dry bulk carriers, sized in deadweight tonnes (dwt). Bigger ships cost less per tonne, but not every port or canal can take them.

Handysize
25–40k dwt · ≈ 180 m
Supramax
50–65k dwt · ≈ 200 m
Panamax
65–82k dwt · ≈ 229 m

Panamax beam (32.3 m) was set by the old Panama Canal locks. Most deep-sea corn sails on Supramax and Panamax ships.

Sheet 07 / 08Trade flows

Main routes

  1. AUS Gulf → Panama Canal → Japan, Korea, China
  2. BUS Gulf → Mexico, the largest US buyer
  3. CUS Pacific Northwest → North Asia
  4. DSantos / Paranaguá → Cape of Good Hope → Singapore → China
  5. EArgentina Up-River → Southeast Asia
  6. FBlack Sea → Bosphorus → Egypt, Spain

Low water in the Panama Canal (2023) pushed US cargo onto longer routes and raised freight.

Sheet 08 / 08End use · US

What it becomes

About a third of the US crop becomes fuel ethanol, and the leftover grain solids (DDGS) go back into feed. Wet mills split the kernel into starch, sweeteners such as HFCS, and corn oil.
